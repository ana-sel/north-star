## 1. Mission — know where you are going

> *"Without a destination, a ship is guided only by whichever wind happens to blow."*

Without mission, the agenda is set by urgency, other people's expectations, and noise. Mission answers: *what kind of life and contribution deserves priority?*

**Five questions to find yours:**

1. What do I want to change in the world, or in my corner of it?
2. Which activity gives me meaning and energy simultaneously?
3. What do I do unusually well — what results speak for themselves?
4. Who benefits from this? Would they value or pay for it?
5. What would still feel important if status and recognition disappeared?

**Mission sits at the intersection of:**
- what you love doing
- what you do well
- what people need
- what they are willing to value
- what gives your life meaning

**Step by step:**

1. Write one paragraph answering the five questions above.
2. Distil it into a single sentence you can read in ten seconds.
3. Use it as a filter: before adding a new project or habit, ask — *does this serve my direction, or does it distract from it?*
4. Review and revise once every 90 days. Mission clarifies through action, not through more thinking.

> *"I did not come into this world for nothing."* — Use this phrase as a daily reminder that your abilities exist for a purpose. Each morning, name one act that serves the purpose.

## 2. Planning — make your life visible

> *"Planning is not a longer to-do list. It is a visible system that converts mission, dreams, roles, projects, and daily actions into a manageable flow."*

**The core formula:**
> Mission → roles → goals → projects → next actions → visible flow → completion → review → improvement

### Step 1 — Empty the head

Write everything down: ideas, promises, worries, half-formed plans, obligations, and aspirations. The first goal is completeness, not order. *What remains invisible is unlikely to be realized.*

### Step 2 — Clarify, don't just collect

For each captured item, decide:
- **Delete** — it is not worth doing.
- **Someday** — park it without guilt.
- **Delegate** — identify who and by when.
- **Quick action** — do it now if it takes under two minutes.
- **Project** — it needs more than one step; create a card.
- **Connect upward** — link it to a goal or role.

### Step 3 — See your life through roles

You act simultaneously as professional, parent, partner, learner, friend, and individual. For each role, define:
- Desired condition
- Current responsibility
- One meaningful result this month
- Minimum maintenance
- Boundaries that protect other roles

*One role must not silently consume the entire person.*

### Step 4 — Build a flow board

Create columns:
**Captured → Clarified → Ready → This Week → In Progress → Waiting → Done**

Set a strict work-in-progress limit: one complex task, one or two simple ones. Finish before opening more.

### Step 5 — Connect work to goals

Twice a month, check every active card: *does this serve a goal, or has it become independent bureaucracy?* Remove what has no justified connection.

### Step 6 — Review statistically

Monthly, ask:
- Which roles received real attention?
- What completed? What repeatedly stalled?
- How many commitments entered versus left the system?
- Where was capacity overestimated?
- What should I stop, delegate, or simplify?

> *"The board is a mirror of thought. Congestion reveals overcommitment or weak decisions."*

## 3. Energy — protect the source before everything else

> *"Time, knowledge, and opportunity are useless when a person lacks the force to act."*

Energy has three connected levels. Weak physical energy drains the higher ones:

1. **Physical** — the base: sleep, food, movement, recovery
2. **Emotional** — engagement, joy, resilience, quality of relationships
3. **Spiritual** — meaning, direction, connection to mission

### The six drains — identify yours first

| Drain | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| Unhealthy lifestyle | Insufficient sleep, poor nutrition, no movement |
| Fear and inner pretence | Chronic worry, anxious thoughts, performing rather than being |
| Open loops and broken commitments | Unfinished tasks the mind keeps revisiting |
| Draining relationships | Meetings or people that reliably leave you empty |
| Passive dependencies | Compulsive scrolling, entertainment, substances |
| Uncontrolled reactions | Letting external events govern your inner state |

> *"You cannot fill the system while there is a large leak. Close the leak first."*

### Seven-day energy audit

For one week, record after each significant activity:
- State before
- State after
- Mark: `+` (gained energy) / `0` (neutral) / `−` (lost energy)

After seven days: strengthen the `+` sources, close the `−` loops, and remove the largest drain first.

### Energy labels for your tasks

Every meaningful block of work carries an energy demand:
- **H (High):** strategy, deep writing, difficult decisions, learning
- **M (Medium):** meetings, standard work, analysis
- **L (Low):** routine, administration, simple calls
- **R (Recovery):** sleep, walking, silence, family, nature

*Match the demand of the task to the energy available at that time of day.*

> *"Two free hours are not equal if one contains clarity and strength while the other contains exhaustion."*

## 4. Morning — the hour that shapes the day

> *"The beginning of a process shapes its continuation. A deliberate morning warms up the body, mind, emotion, and intention before the demands of the day arrive."*

> *"Life consists of individual days. Change the beginning of each day, and you gradually change the whole life."*

### Prepare the night before

Early rising without timely sleep produces exhaustion, not benefit. The morning starts the evening before:

- Decide on a sleep time and protect it
- Remove screens 30–60 minutes before bed
- Prepare clothes, shoes, and materials for tomorrow
- Write one question you want your morning mind to sit with
- Leave the phone out of reach from the bed

*The goal is not to beat the alarm clock — it is to create a calm enough start.*

**How to shift gradually:** Add 15 minutes, adapt for one to two weeks, then add another 15. Never cut sleep below your actual need. Watch for the signs of cutting too much: irritability, poor concentration, reaching for coffee to compensate.

### Step 1 — Mental rehearsal (2 minutes, still in bed)

Before getting up, run through the day in your mind. Rehearsal does not require the day to follow one script — it prepares your responses and reduces anxiety.

Cover five points:
1. The principal event of the day
2. Your desired behaviour in it
3. The most likely obstacle
4. Your chosen response to it
5. An acceptable alternative outcome

### Step 2 — Four orienting phrases

These phrases work not as a magic spell but as **attention cues** — each one redirects how you see the coming day. Say each phrase, then name one concrete action that follows from it.

| Phrase | What it does | Action to attach |
|---|---|---|
| *"I am happy and content with life."* | Returns attention to what already exists | Name one thing you genuinely have |
| *"The world is full of abundance and miracles."* | Opens perception to possibility and unexpected openings | Notice one thing today you didn't plan for |
| *"What happens to me follows my intentions."* | Connects outer events to inner direction | Name your true intention before the first important action |
| *"I am responsible for everything that happens to me."* | Returns the power to change | Accept responsibility for one open situation |

*Without a concrete action, a phrase remains only words.*

### Step 3 — Walk for at least 30 minutes

Walking combines movement, daylight, breathing, and unstructured thought. Protect both halves:

**First half — silence.** No podcasts, no music, no messages. Let the mind settle. This is not wasted time; it is the clearing that makes the next part useful.

**Second half — Kaizen questions.** Use the walk as a gentle planning review:
- What is most important today?
- What can be made simpler or more effective?
- What obstacle is likely? What is my response?
- Which conversation cannot be postponed?
- What does *not* need to be done today?

*Do not turn the walk into another stream of content.*

### Step 4 — Breakfast and transition

After the walk: a simple healthy breakfast, then move to the principal task before opening other people's messages — if circumstances allow. The first hour of work is the most valuable. Protect it from incoming requests.

### Minimum version for a difficult day

A compressed morning is better than no morning at all:
- 1 minute of gratitude
- 2 minutes of mental rehearsal
- 10 minutes of walking
- Write one priority for the day

### Evaluate after 14 days

After two weeks, review each element honestly. Keep only the parts that actually improve your day. Drop the rest without guilt.

Track daily:
| What to observe | Good sign | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep quality | Rested on waking | Needing coffee immediately |
| Energy before midday | Clear and willing | Heavy or irritable |
| Principal task | Done before noon | Displaced by incoming requests |
| Mood | Stable, available | Reactive, scattered |
| Phone in the morning | Opened after the walk | First thing before getting up |

**Rule:** Earlier is useful only when sleep remains sufficient. *An incomplete practice is better than none at all.*

## 5. Habits — the one practice that multiplies everything else

> *"One hour of improvement multiplies the value of all remaining hours."*

**The Kaizen hour** is the single most leveraged habit: one daily period reserved for improving *how* you live and work. Ordinary work produces today's output. The Kaizen hour improves the method used by hundreds of future hours.

Use a walk or uninterrupted sitting to ask:

- What should I do differently?
- What can be made simpler or more effective?
- Which mistake must not recur?
- What should I learn, stop, delegate, or automate?
- Are my current goals still correct?

### Convert experience into algorithms

When something goes wrong, write a rule beginning with *"How to…"*:
- How to decline a request without damaging the relationship
- How to prepare for a recurring meeting
- How to respond when my energy is low and a decision is needed

> *"A captured lesson becomes an algorithm. An algorithm becomes a reusable resource."*

**Two types of mistake to avoid at all costs:**
1. Mistakes with irreversible consequences when a safer test was possible
2. The same mistake repeated without learning

### Habit review — weekly questions

Use the Kaizen hour once a week for this:
- Is this goal still mine? Does it serve my mission?
- Which metric is actually moving?
- What is the current constraint?
- What single action gives the most return?
- What should I stop?

**Suggested weekly rhythm for the Kaizen hour:**
| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Monday | Principal priority |
| Tuesday | Process and friction |
| Wednesday | Learning and skill |
| Thursday | Relationships and communication |
| Friday | Mistakes and algorithms |
| Weekend | Goals, energy, and life as a whole |

## 6. Focus — growth goes where attention goes

> *"You achieve the goals on which you actually maintain focus — not the goals you merely declare."*

Everything is either growing or degrading. There is no neutral. Growth happens where sustained attention is directed.

**The four-step growth algorithm:**

1. **Define the dimension** — not "improve," but a specific area: income, a skill, a relationship, physical capacity, emotional steadiness.
2. **Choose a metric** — what number or observable result demonstrates real movement?
3. **Identify direct actions** — for each task, ask: *"If I do this consistently, will it directly move the metric?"* Separate these from maintenance, administration, and noise.
4. **Give direct actions your best attention** — use the highest-energy period of the day for them. Handle indirect and urgent work afterward.

**Daily practice:**

*Morning (2 minutes):*
1. What exactly am I growing right now?
2. What is my metric?
3. What one action most directly moves it today?
4. When will I do it, without interruption?

*Evening (2 minutes):*
- What actually received most of my attention?
- Did that serve growth?
- What tried to steal focus?
- What will I remove or rearrange tomorrow?

> *"Urgency must not automatically outrank importance."*

## 7. Efficiency — find your point of effort

> *"Results depend less on the quantity of effort than on where it is applied."*

Working harder at the wrong point does not produce proportionate returns. Intelligent work is finding the small number of factors that determine most of the outcome.

**Three tools:**

**1. Identify the key success factor**
Ask: *what single condition most increases the probability of success here?*
In one context it may be location; in another, trust, distribution, product quality, or access to decision-makers.

**2. Apply the 80/20 principle**
Roughly 20% of activities create 80% of results. Measure which customers, actions, or habits genuinely move the metric. Reduce low-impact work.

**3. Find the current constraint**
The system is limited by its weakest link. Direct 80% of focused effort at removing that link. Once it moves, find the next one.

**Practical sequence:**
1. Define the desired result.
2. List all recurring activities.
3. Measure their real contribution.
4. Name the key success factor.
5. Find the bottleneck.
6. Allocate concentrated effort there.
7. Delegate, automate, simplify, or stop low-impact work.
8. When the constraint moves, reassess.

> *"Correct point × concentrated effort = disproportionate result."*

## 8. Decision-making — a practical framework

> *"Before making any large investment, confirm the audience and the demand in practice."*

### Principle 1 — Clarify intention before acting

Before an important decision, ask:
- What do I truly intend?
- Whom should this benefit?
- Is the visible goal aligned with the motive beneath it?
- Would I respect the person this intention makes me?

> *"People judge deeds. Judge your intentions before your deeds."*

### Principle 2 — Test before committing

A hypothesis is a belief in testable form:
> *"I believe this specific person/group has this problem and will respond to this solution."*

Before building fully, test cheaply: a conversation, a prototype, a small offer, a pre-order. *Measure real behaviour, not compliments.*

### Principle 3 — One experiment at a time

Explore broadly until evidence appears. Then concentrate fully on the strongest result. Constantly switching prevents the depth that compounds advantage.

> *"Explore broadly until you have evidence. Then exploit the strongest result with full focus."*

### Principle 4 — Avoid two types of mistake

1. **Irreversible mistakes** when a safer test was available — never take an unrecoverable risk without checking first.
2. **Repeated mistakes** without a new lesson — every error that recurs is a sign the algorithm was not written or not used.

### Principle 5 — Accept responsibility to regain power

When something goes wrong, replace *"Who must change?"* with:
- How did I participate in this outcome?
- What did I fail to notice?
- What can I do now?
- What habit or decision prevents repetition?

*Responsibility is not self-blame. It is locating the next useful action within your own control.*

## 9. Self-care and recovery — a condition, not a reward

> *"Recovery should begin before collapse, not after it."*

Recovery is not a bonus for finishing all the work. It is the condition under which the work remains possible.

### The battery scale

| Level | State |
|---|---|
| 80–100 | Strong, clear, creative |
| 60–79 | Normal working range |
| 40–59 | Reduced quality; use medium or low demands only |
| 20–39 | Recovery required before continuing |
| 0–19 | The system has been neglected; cancel non-essential demands |

**Rule: never let yourself reach 7%.** Recovery begins at 40, not at 0.

### Build the day from anchors, not fragments

Before filling the calendar with tasks, place:
1. Sufficient sleep
2. Strongest personal work period
3. Meals and movement
4. Recovery after predictable intensity
5. Meaningful relationship time
6. A closing boundary for work

*Then* add meetings and routine. Recovery receives a reserved slot, not whatever remains.

### Recovery menu (prepared in advance)

| Duration | Options |
|---|---|
| 5 minutes | Breathing, water, step outside, stretch, eyes closed |
| 20 minutes | Walk, short rest, quiet meal, shower, enjoyable conversation |
| 1–3 hours | Nature, exercise, hobby, family, full information break |

Choose recovery according to the depletion. Social exhaustion may need solitude; loneliness may need good people.

### Weekly energy audit

At the end of each week, review:
- When was energy highest? What produced it?
- Which people replenished or drained it?
- What work returned energy after completion?
- What did I consume that left me weaker?
- What will I adjust next week?

## 10. Character and responsibility — the inner operating system

> *"Do not build a life before knowing whose life you are trying to build."*

These are not principles to perform for others. They are orientations that, repeated daily, gradually shape how you see and respond to the world.

**Four phrases — say them and attach an action to each:**

| Phrase | Daily action |
|---|---|
| *"I did not come into this world for nothing."* | Name one act today that serves your purpose. |
| *"The world is full of miracles."* | Notice one unexpected opening or helpful coincidence. |
| *"What happens to me follows my intentions."* | Before an important action, name your true intention. |
| *"I am responsible for everything that happens to me."* | Accept responsibility for one situation instead of assigning blame. |

**Self-knowledge questions — revisit quarterly:**
- What desires, fears, and motivations actually govern my choices?
- Which talents produce better results than my peers, confirmed by outcomes?
- What do I love, what do I do well, what do people need, and what gives my life meaning?
- Do I want to grow more quickly — and if so, what exactly, and how is it measured?

**Rules for a strong character:**
- Do not scatter the force of youth or current energy.
- Address problems while they are small; ignored problems grow.
- Make self-development goal-directed: learn what the next result requires, then apply it immediately.
- Build necessary relationships before a crisis makes them urgent.
- Treat social skill — clarity, listening, negotiation, reliability, conflict resolution — as a core life competency.
- Protect attention as the principal resource of development.

> *"Focused attention + targeted development + strong relationships + intelligent failure + mission = accelerated maturity."*

## How to use this guide

**Don't read it all at once.** Read one section, sit with it for a week, and practise that one area. Then move forward.

A useful sequence for someone starting:
1. Week 1 — Write your mission draft (section 1).
2. Week 2 — Empty your head and build a simple flow board (section 2).
3. Week 3 — Do the seven-day energy audit (section 3).
4. Week 4 — Establish a morning routine, even the minimum version (section 4).
5. Month 2 — Begin the Kaizen hour habit (section 5).
6. Month 3 onwards — Apply focus, efficiency, and decision frameworks to one active goal.

> *"A compass, not a map. Use it to orient yourself — then walk."*
