# Plan Tab — Kaizen Planning Foundations

> Design reference spec for the Compass **Plan** tab.  
> Sources: *Kaizen Planning as Foundation of a Happy Life* · *Kaizen Planning vs To-Do List* · *Kaizen Planning 2.0 + Power Management* · *Kaizen in Japan and Toyota's Success* · *Как планировать свою жизнь и дела | Кайдзен планирование 2.0*

---

## 1. Core Teachings (distilled)

### Philosophy in 5 bullets

- **Planning is not a longer to-do list.** It is a *visible system* that converts mission, dreams, thoughts, roles, projects, habits, and daily actions into a manageable flow. The goal is not to hold more — it is to move more.
- **Kaizen planning is a continuous flow, not a snapshot.** A to-do list treats time as a fixed container (a glass of water). Kaizen treats time as a pipe: work enters, moves through stages, and exits as completed outcomes. You never "finish" the system — you improve it.
- **The source of all planning is mission (ikigai).** Without a clear why, urgency and other people's expectations fill the schedule. Mission is the only filter that lets you say *no* with confidence and *yes* with energy.
- **Roles protect the whole life.** Every person is simultaneously a parent, partner, professional, friend, learner, and individual. Planning that ignores roles silently bankrupts some of them. The system must show which roles received attention this week.
- **Completions, not tasks, are the unit of effectiveness.** The measure is not how many items are on a list. It is how many meaningful results were finished. A plan that creates the *feeling* of being busy without producing completions is broken.

### Key metaphors and mental models

| Metaphor | What it means |
|---|---|
| **The pipe / flow** | Planning is a stream, not a container. Work enters raw and exits completed. Blockages are visible and diagnosable. |
| **The messy room** | A to-do list is like walking into a chaotic room and being told "clean it". Kaizen breaks it into: *clear this one corner today, completely*. |
| **1 hour of planning = 10 days of work** | Margulan's ratio: 1 focused planning hour compresses 10 days of reactive, unplanned effort. |
| **The board as a mirror** | A kanban board reflects the mind. Crowded columns = overcommitment or weak decisions. Empty "done" = energy going in but nothing coming out. |
| **Ikigai** | Japanese concept from Okinawa's long-lived communities: *iki* = life, *gai* = worth/reason. The reason you wake up. Not a single mission statement — the intersection of what you love, what you're good at, what the world needs, and what sustains you. |
| **Shukunin** | Japanese master craftsman. A street sweeper who wants to be the *best street sweeper in the world* — not head of the sweeping department. Depth over breadth; mastery of your own lane. |
| **Toyota's visual standard** | Every part of the system is made *visible*: what should be here, how many, which stage, is something off. Deviation is immediately noticeable. Applied to life: the planning board must show you *where you are* without needing to remember. |

## 2. The Framework / Method (step-by-step)

### The master formula

```
Mission → Roles → Goals (90-day) → Projects → Next Actions → Visible Flow → Completion → Review → Improvement
```

### Stage 1: Mission (the filter)

Ask five questions. The answers filter everything that enters the planning system:

1. **What do I want to change** in the world or my community?
2. **Which activity gives me meaning and energy** — not just money?
3. **What do I do unusually well** that others notice?
4. **Who benefits** when I do this work?
5. **What would remain important** even if there were no status or money attached?

*Rule:* Tasks that cannot be justified by any of these five answers are candidates for deletion. Not optimization — deletion.

### Stage 2: Roles (the life map)

List all the life roles currently active. Typical roles: parent · partner · professional · friend · citizen · learner · self.

For each role define:
- **Desired condition:** What does "good" look like in this role in 90 days?
- **Current responsibility:** What is already committed?
- **One meaningful result:** The single outcome that would most improve this role.
- **Minimum maintenance:** What must happen to keep this role from deteriorating?
- **Boundaries:** What will NOT be accepted in this role?

*Rule when planning a week:* Each role should appear at least once. A role with zero appearance for two weeks is being silently neglected.

### Stage 3: Goals (90-day horizon)

Map goals to roles. Limit: 1–3 active goals per role. More than this and none will be completed.

For each goal:
- State the **finished result** (not "work on health" → "run 5km without stopping by day 90")
- Name the **key success factor**: the single condition that most determines whether this goal is achieved
- Assign a **90-day deadline**

### Stage 4: Projects (multi-action commitments)

Everything that requires more than one action is a project. "Learn Arabic" is a project. "Look up Arabic course options" is an action.

Break every project into **visible increments** — chunks with:
- a clear finish line
- small enough to schedule this week
- produces visible progress when done
- supplies feedback (did it work?)
- leads naturally to the next increment

*Example:* "Learn Arabic" → Increment 1: "Complete lessons 1–10 of Duolingo Arabic" → Increment 2: "Learn first 100 words with flashcards" → etc.

### Stage 5: The Flow Board (Kanban)

Use 7 columns:

| Column | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Inbox** | Raw captured thoughts, commitments, ideas — unprocessed |
| **Clarify** | Items where the finished result isn't yet defined |
| **Ready** | Specific next actions that can be started immediately |
| **This week** | Consciously chosen commitment for this week |
| **In progress** | Actually started — hard limit: 1 complex + 1–2 simple |
| **Waiting** | Dependent on another person or event |
| **Done** | Verifiable completion — *never delete these* |

**WIP limit is sacred.** Exceeding it creates the illusion of productivity while producing congestion.

### Stage 6: Card anatomy (2.0 standard)

Each task/project card contains:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Result** | What will be finished (not "work on X" — "X is done") |
| **Link** | Goal, project, or role this serves |
| **Next action** | The physically executable next step |
| **Done when** | Completion criterion — how do you know it's done? |
| **Time estimate** | Realistic duration |
| **Energy level** | High / Medium / Low |
| **Energy type** | Physical / Emotional / Intellectual / Spiritual |
| **Deadline** | Only if a real external date exists |
| **Owner** | One person responsible |

*Never name cards "Marketing", "Health", "Family".* Write the finished result.

### Stage 7: Review (the improvement loop)

**Weekly review:**
- Which roles got attention? Which were neglected?
- How many cards moved to Done?
- What stalled, and why?
- What enters next week that aligns with mission?
- What can be deleted, delegated, or deferred?

**Monthly review (statistical):**
- Completions per week (track trend)
- Average cycle time (start → done)
- Which role was over/under-served?
- Which goal moved? Which stalled?
- What type of work created energy vs exhaustion?
- One improvement to the system itself

*Rule:* The system improves through evidence, not through willpower. If something keeps stalling, the system has a design problem — not the person.

## 3. Design Rules for the Plan Tab

> **Rule: Mission-first entry**  
> **Principle:** The first time a user opens Plan, prompt them to capture a mission statement before any tasks or goals.  
> **Rationale:** Without mission, planning defaults to urgency and others' expectations (Margulan). Every subsequent goal is filtered against this anchor. SDT research confirms that intrinsic motivation (autonomy + meaning) produces sustainable behavior; extrinsic task lists do not.

> **Rule: Flow visibility over list length**  
> **Principle:** Never show all tasks in a flat list. Always show work as movement through stages (at minimum: to do → in progress → done).  
> **Rationale:** The flat to-do list is cognitively overwhelming — the brain sees an undifferentiated mass and chooses avoidance (Kahneman System 1: avoid ambiguous overwhelm). Flow boards trigger progress perception, which is one of the strongest intrinsic motivators (Teresa Amabile's Progress Principle).

> **Rule: WIP limits are non-negotiable**  
> **Principle:** Enforce a maximum of 1 item "in progress" (complex) + 2 items (simple) at any time. Surface gentle friction when the user tries to exceed this.  
> **Rationale:** Margulan: "If you can complete three meaningful things per day, planning twenty doesn't create more capacity." Cognitive load theory: parallel open loops consume working memory. Toyota's kanban: throughput improves by finishing, not starting.

> **Rule: Incremental decomposition, not a big plan**  
> **Principle:** Goals should be breakable into the smallest visible next increment inside the app — not planned all the way to completion upfront.  
> **Rationale:** "Learn Arabic" cannot be executed. Planning it all the way to fluency before starting is a form of procrastination. Margulan's messy-room example: clear one corner completely, then choose the next corner. BJ Fogg: the smallest possible version of a behavior is easiest to start.

> **Rule: Roles as planning lenses**  
> **Principle:** The Plan tab should allow users to filter their board by role (parent / professional / self / etc.) and surface which roles have received no attention this week.  
> **Rationale:** Margulan: planning that doesn't preserve the whole life silently bankrupts some roles. Without role visibility, users optimize one domain (usually work) while neglecting others.

> **Rule: Completions are the primary metric**  
> **Principle:** The app should celebrate and surface completions — not task count, streak count, or productivity score.  
> **Rationale:** The correct unit of a planning system is completed meaningful results. Measuring tasks recorded or hours worked rewards input, not output. Margulan explicitly: "Effectiveness is measured by completed valuable work — not by the number of recorded tasks."

> **Rule: Review is built into the rhythm**  
> **Principle:** Weekly and monthly review prompts should be a first-class feature, not an optional setting. The review is what turns the board into a learning system.  
> **Rationale:** Toyota principle: without a standard and measurement, you cannot improve. Margulan's kaizen loop only works if the retrospective happens. Without review, the board is a graveyard of old intentions.

> **Rule: The board shows "where I am", not "what I've failed to do"**  
> **Principle:** Language in the Plan tab should never imply failure for incomplete items. Stalled tasks should be framed as "needs a decision" not "overdue".  
> **Rationale:** A to-do list demoralizes (Margulan). Compass principle: no shame, no punishment. SDT: shame destroys autonomous motivation. Neff self-compassion research: self-criticism impairs performance over time.

## 4. Psychology & Burnout Layer

### BJ Fogg — Tiny Habits
Fogg's research: behavior change requires (a) motivation, (b) ability, (c) prompt — in that order. The smallest executable version of a goal is always the right starting point. The messy-room example is Fogg's "tiny habit" expressed as a planning metaphor: *don't clean the room — clear one shelf, celebrate the shelf*.

**App implication:** When a user adds a big goal ("get fit", "launch business"), the app should prompt decomposition into the smallest first action — and celebrate completing that one action.

### Kahneman — System 1 & System 2
A flat to-do list triggers System 1 (automatic) anxiety response — too much, too complex, no clear start. A flow board with visible stages engages System 2 (deliberate) thinking. The WIP limit forces deliberate prioritization.

**App implication:** The default view should never show more items than can be comfortably held in working memory (~3–5 per column). Use progressive disclosure for the backlog.

### SDT — Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)
Three needs: autonomy (choosing), competence (succeeding), relatedness (connection to something larger). Mission anchoring satisfies relatedness. WIP limits and achievable increments satisfy competence. User-chosen priorities satisfy autonomy.

**App implication:** Never auto-assign goals or use algorithmic "suggested tasks". Let the user define their own mission, roles, and priorities.

### Csikszentmihalyi — Flow
Flow emerges when challenge ≈ skill. Too easy = boredom; too hard = anxiety. Kaizen's incremental approach calibrates challenge: each increment is designed to be just beyond comfortable but within reach.

**App implication:** As users complete increments, subtly increase the ambition of the next suggested increment. Never dump the whole difficulty at once.

### James Clear — Atomic Habits
Clear's 2% improvement rule is Kaizen applied to habits. Small daily improvements compound exponentially. The identity shift ("I am a person who plans") is more durable than goal-chasing ("I want to lose weight").

**App implication:** Plan tab should reinforce identity ("you are someone who follows through") not just goal progress. Language: "You completed another step on your [role] path" not "task 3/10 done".

### Burnout research (Maslach, Leiter)
Burnout is caused by: chronic overload, lack of control, insufficient reward, community breakdown, unfairness, and values conflict. Kaizen planning directly addresses the first three: WIP limits reduce overload; visible progress creates reward; mission alignment prevents values conflict.

**Anti-burnout design rule:** The plan review should regularly surface energy data alongside completion data. If a user has high completion but declining energy scores, the system should gently suggest reducing load or adding recovery.

### Ancient wisdom parallels

| Tradition | Parallel |
|---|---|
| **Japanese Kaizen / Zen** | The idea that mastery comes from doing the ordinary extraordinarily well, every day, without end. "Perfect the ordinary." The board is a zendo: clear, orderly, nothing unnecessary. |
| **Taoist wu wei** | Effortless action — not laziness, but working with natural flow rather than against resistance. WIP limits honor natural capacity. Forcing 20 tasks into a day is *wei* (forced action). |
| **Buddhist Middle Way** | Neither excess nor deprivation. Not maximum productivity, not inaction. The kaizen plan finds the sustainable rhythm between the two. |
| **Stoic dichotomy of control** | Epictetus: "Some things are in our control and others not." The plan should focus on *actions* (in our control) and not on outcomes (partly not in our control). Margulan's "line of most effective behavior" is a Stoic formulation. |
| **Aristotle's hexis (habituation)** | Virtues are built by repeated action, not by grand decisions. A person becomes disciplined by doing disciplined things daily, not by willing themselves to be disciplined. Kaizen embodies this. |

## 5. Anti-patterns to Avoid

### Margulan's explicit warnings → Design anti-patterns

| Warning from transcript | App anti-pattern to avoid |
|---|---|
| "A long to-do list paralyzes before you even start" | Never show the full backlog by default. Backlog is a separate, intentionally hidden view. |
| "Planning 20 tasks when you can complete 3 is not more planning — it's self-deception" | Don't let users add unlimited tasks to "This Week". Surface a gentle signal at 5+ items. |
| "Without mission, the calendar fills with urgency and other people's priorities" | Don't allow first-use without a mission capture prompt. |
| "Kaizen is never loyal to the tool — only to living more clearly" | Don't make the board the hero. Make completions and clarity the hero. If the board isn't helping, make it easy to simplify or restructure. |
| "A standard before improvement — without a known method, you can't evaluate change" | Weekly review must include a reflection on the *planning system itself*, not just results. |
| "Improvement cannot be delegated — the person performing the work is closest to the friction" | Don't algorithmically reorganize or "optimize" users' plans. The user decides what moves and what stays. AI can suggest, never impose. |

### Additional design anti-patterns (from psychology)

- **Shame triggers:** Never show "X days overdue" — this is a blame framing. Show "needs a decision" or simply the date the card last moved.
- **Completion theater:** Don't make it easy to mark things "done" without real completion (the temptation to move cards to feel productive). The card's "done when" criterion should be visible at the point of completion.
- **Motivation inflation:** Don't reward streaks for *planning* (e.g. "you planned for 7 days in a row!"). Reward completions and reflection, not ritual.
- **Goal avalanche:** Don't offer a "goal library" or "popular goals" — this pushes external comparison and social pressure. Mission and goals must come entirely from the user.

## 6. Voice & Tone Notes

### How Margulan frames struggle and setbacks

Margulan is warm, practical, and *never dramatic* about struggle. He normalizes it:
> *"This happened to me many times. I lost a lot on this. That's why I want to share it with you."*

He frames setbacks as **data, not failure**:
> *"The system exposes where work stopped. That's a gift — now you can fix the right thing."*

He never says "you failed" — he says "the system has a problem" or "the decision wasn't made yet".

### Tone for the Plan tab (copy and micro-interactions)

**When a user skips a weekly review:**
- ✗ "You haven't reviewed your goals in 7 days"
- ✓ "Ready to see what moved this week?" (or just: a soft badge that disappears if dismissed)

**When a card has been stalled for 2 weeks:**
- ✗ "Overdue"
- ✓ "This hasn't moved in a while — delegate, defer, or decide?"

**When the WIP limit is reached:**
- ✗ "You can't add more tasks"
- ✓ "Your hands are full. Finish something first, or move it back to Ready."

**When a completion is logged:**
- ✓ Short, warm: "Done. That moved [role name] forward."
- Don't: confetti, badges, streaks, or "level up" language

**When a goal is abandoned:**
- ✓ "Pausing this? It'll stay here — nothing is lost."
- ✓ Completion is *self-claimed*: "Does this feel done for now?" — not graded by the app

**Welcome back after a gap:**
- ✓ "Welcome back. Let's see where things stand."
- ✗ Any reference to the gap, the streak, or what was missed

## 7. Implementation notes for the Plan tab screens

### plan-month.html (monthly view)
Should show: role distribution pie/bar (visual), goals with progress, completion count for the month. The "statistical review" lens. No individual tasks visible here.

### plan-horizon.html (90-day / goal level)
Should show: goals per role, each goal's key success factor, progress toward the goal, linked projects. This is the strategy lens.

### plan-map.html (mission + life map)
Should show: mission statement, role cards with desired condition vs current, balance wheel visualization. This is the *why* and *who* lens — it anchors everything else. Users return here when they feel lost or burned out.

### Kanban board (no current screen — recommend adding plan-board.html)
Should show: the flow board with columns and WIP limit. This is the *doing* lens — what moves today and this week.
