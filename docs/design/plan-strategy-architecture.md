# Plan Tab — Strategy and Goal Architecture

> Design reference spec for the Compass **Plan** tab.  
> Sources: *What is Strategy? Understand and Achieve Anything in Life* · *Time Clustering: An Effective Way to Plan Your Day* · *Find Your Point of Effort (80/20 and Leverage Point)*

---

## 1. Core Teachings (distilled)

### Philosophy in 5 bullets

- **Strategy is a line of behavior, not a list of plans.** Most people confuse "strategy" with "plan". A plan is a sequence of tasks. A strategy is the *consistent pattern of behavior* that concentrates your attention on what matters most over the long term.
- **Any action not based on strategy is just bustle before failure.** Margulan's core warning: busyness without strategy produces the *feeling* of productivity with very few actual results.
- **Results come from targeted, precise action — not from random effort.** The more chaotic and scattered the effort, the less it produces. Direction multiplies force.
- **Time clustering increases effectiveness by 25–30% from one change alone.** Switching between dissimilar tasks costs significant re-entry time. Grouping similar work into blocks recovers this loss.
- **The 80/20 principle applies to your own life.** Roughly 20% of your activities produce 80% of your meaningful results. The planning system must identify which activities those are, and protect them from being crowded out by the other 80%.

### Key metaphors and mental models

| Metaphor | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Three generals** | A Chinese parable Margulan uses: the first general fights without a plan (pure instinct). The second builds maximum defenses (reactive fortress). The third says: *hold the high ground, control the water supply, use terrain and timing*. That third general has strategy — he shapes conditions before the battle, rather than reacting during it. |
| **Strategy = planned line of most effective behavior** | Margulan's definition: a strategy is a planned line of the most effective behavior for achieving your goals, through concentrating attention on what has the greatest long-term importance. Not a destination — a *direction of travel*. |
| **Time as a pipe, not a box** | A calendar that just books hours treats time as a box to fill. Time clustering treats time as a flow — you shape it in advance by pre-allocating blocks of similar-energy work, not just filling empty slots. |
| **The "balance wheel" (колесо баланса)** | A common life wheel with sectors: work, family, health, hobbies, learning, finance, etc. Margulan's critique: the wheel alone does nothing. You must assign a *time block* to each sector. Without resource allocation, balance is a wish, not a design. |
| **Point of application (точка приложения усилий)** | From physics: the same force applied at the correct fulcrum point moves far more than force applied randomly. In life: the same effort applied to the right activity produces disproportionate results. |

## 2. The Framework / Method (step-by-step)

### What is Strategy: the full breakdown

Margulan's definition, unpacked into its parts:

**1. "Planned line"** — strategy is deliberate. It is not improvisation, not "we'll figure it out on the battlefield". It is thought through in advance.

**2. "Most effective behavior"** — not any behavior. Not the most effortful behavior. The *most effective* one. Effectiveness means: maximum result per unit of effort, in the direction that matters.

**3. "For achieving your goals"** — it must be connected to actual goals. Strategy without goals is wandering. Goals without strategy are wishes.

**4. "Through concentrating attention"** — strategy is as much about what you *don't* do as what you do. Concentration requires saying no to alternatives that seem good but are not the best.

**5. "On what has the greatest long-term importance"** — the long-term qualifier is critical. Many things feel urgent short-term but matter little in the long run. Strategy inverts this: protect the long-term important even when it's not urgent.

### The three generals model: identifying your strategic posture

Before planning goals, identify which posture you're operating from:

| General | Posture | Planning style | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **General 1** | "We'll figure it out" | Reactive, instinct-driven | No leverage, exhausted by constant firefighting |
| **General 2** | "Build maximum defenses" | Defensive, fortress mode | Protects what exists but rarely advances |
| **General 3 (strategic)** | "Control conditions" | Proactive, shapes the terrain | Requires discipline but compounds over time |

*Rule for the app:* Help users move from General 1/2 to General 3 thinking. The Plan tab's role is to surface *condition-shaping* opportunities, not just task execution.

### Time Clustering: the method

**What it is:** Pre-allocating blocks of the week to similar types of activity, so that context-switching costs are minimized and each block benefits from accumulated focus.

**Why it works:**
- Scientists have demonstrated that switching between dissimilar tasks costs re-entry time — you have to "reload" context each time
- Each interruption in a task means re-reading, re-thinking, re-orientating — this happens every single time
- Simply implementing clustering raises personal effectiveness by 25–30%
- Additionally: clustering creates *guaranteed time for each life sector* — it transforms the balance wheel from a hope into a structural commitment

**The clustering method (Margulan's version):**

1. **Map your roles and activity types** (Deep work / Meetings / Creative / Admin / Family / Health / Learning / Finance)
2. **Assign each type a color or label**
3. **Block the week in advance** by type, not by specific task
   - Example: Monday 09:00–12:00 = Deep work block (strategy, writing, complex decisions)
   - Monday 14:00–16:00 = Meetings/calls cluster
   - Tuesday morning = Creative/learning block
   - Friday 17:00–18:00 = Weekly review + planning block
4. **Protect the blocks like appointments** — they are not suggestions
5. **Within each block, do only that type of work** — no mixing
6. **Never schedule without knowing the energy type** the block requires

**The re-entry cost principle:**
> When you interrupt a task to switch to another, you must pay the re-entry cost when you return: re-reading, re-contextualizing, re-entering the mental state. Clustering eliminates these costs.

### The 80/20 Point of Application method

**When to use it:** When a goal is stalled, when effort feels disproportionate to results, or when deciding what to stop doing.

**Step-by-step:**

1. **Define the desired result** clearly (not "grow the business" — "increase monthly revenue by X")
2. **List all recurring activities** related to this result
3. **Measure their actual contribution** (even rough estimates count — the pattern matters)
4. **Name the key success factor:** the single condition that, if improved, most increases probability of achieving the result
   - For a restaurant: location and foot traffic
   - For a personal brand: consistency of content + distribution channel
   - For learning: daily practice + quality feedback
5. **Find the current bottleneck:** what is the *constraint* that, if removed, allows everything else to flow faster?
6. **Allocate 80% of focused effort to the key factor/bottleneck**
7. **Delegate, automate, simplify, or stop** the low-impact 80%
8. **Reassess after the constraint moves** — the bottleneck will shift, and a new point of application emerges

**Formula:**
```
Correct point × concentrated effort = disproportionate result
```

**Three ways to find your leverage point:**

| Method | Question |
|---|---|
| Key success factor | What single condition most increases probability of success? |
| 80/20 audit | Which 20% of activities produce 80% of results? |
| Root cause / constraint | What is the one bottleneck that, if removed, unlocks everything else? |

## 3. Design Rules for the Plan Tab

> **Rule: Strategy before goals**  
> **Principle:** Before users set specific goals, prompt them to articulate their strategy for each role — the *line of behavior* they intend to follow, not a list of tasks.  
> **Rationale:** Margulan: goals without strategy are wishes. A strategy forces the user to state *how they intend to behave*, which is a far stronger commitment than a destination alone.

> **Rule: Show the strategic posture**  
> **Principle:** In the Horizon view, each role should show its current strategic posture (reactive → defensive → proactive/condition-shaping). This gives users self-awareness about where they are operating.  
> **Rationale:** General 1 users (reactive) don't know they're reactive. Making posture visible is the first step to changing it. This is Kahneman System 2 activation — it makes the unconscious pattern conscious.

> **Rule: Time clustering as the weekly planning unit**  
> **Principle:** The weekly planning interface should be cluster-first — users assign *types of work* to time blocks before individual tasks. Tasks then live inside their cluster.  
> **Rationale:** Clustering raises personal effectiveness by 25–30% (Margulan cites research). Standard calendar apps don't enforce type-separation. The app should make this structural. This also directly implements the balance-wheel — each life sector gets an allocated time resource, not just a aspiration.

> **Rule: Surface the 80/20 question during goal review**  
> **Principle:** In the monthly or quarterly review, ask: "Which 20% of your actions moved this goal most? What can you reduce or stop?"  
> **Rationale:** Margulan: most effort goes to the low-impact 80%. The only way to recover this capacity is to regularly surface it with a structured question. Without this prompt, users never audit their own activity mix.

> **Rule: The key success factor is mandatory for each goal**  
> **Principle:** A goal card is not complete until the user has named the one factor that most determines success.  
> **Rationale:** This forces strategic thinking before execution. It is Margulan's "General 3" posture: understand the terrain before moving. If users can't name the key factor, the goal is not yet clearly understood.

> **Rule: Block overlap protection**  
> **Principle:** When time clusters of different types are scheduled adjacently, warn the user about re-entry costs (especially mixed cognitive/meeting clusters).  
> **Rationale:** The science on task-switching is clear: mixing deep work with meetings in back-to-back blocks destroys the value of the deep work block. The app should make this visible, not just allow it silently.

> **Rule: Horizon view shows role balance, not task density**  
> **Principle:** The 90-day horizon view should show which roles have goals and which don't — not a count of tasks per role.  
> **Rationale:** A role with no active goal is not necessarily neglected — it may be in a maintenance period. But a role with no *attention allocation* is being neglected. The balance wheel made structural.

## 4. Psychology & Burnout Layer

### Attention economics (Kahneman)
Switching cost is not metaphorical — it is a real cognitive tax. Each context switch requires the prefrontal cortex to reload working memory. Studies estimate 15–23 minutes to return to deep focus after an interruption. Clustering eliminates the interruption pattern entirely.

**Burnout connection:** Constant context-switching is a form of chronic mental fatigue. The fragmented day (the default for most knowledge workers) is structurally exhausting even when the total hours are reasonable.

### Goal-setting theory (Locke & Latham)
Specific, challenging goals with feedback produce higher performance than vague or easy goals. Margulan's "finished result" card format is a direct implementation of specificity. The key success factor adds the challenge dimension — it clarifies what "hard" looks like for this goal.

### Implementation intentions (Gollwitzer)
"If X, then Y" plans dramatically increase goal completion rates. Time clustering is a structural implementation intention: "If it is Monday 9am, then I do deep work." The when-where-what is pre-decided, eliminating decision fatigue at execution time.

### Burnout (Maslach) — the values conflict dimension
One of Burnout's six causes is *values conflict* — when daily work contradicts what the person believes is important. Strategy anchored to personal mission prevents this: if daily actions don't connect to the strategic line, they can be removed. The monthly 80/20 audit is a values-conflict detector.

### Ancient wisdom parallels

| Tradition | Parallel |
|---|---|
| **Sun Tzu** | "Strategy without tactics is the slowest route to victory. Tactics without strategy is the noise before defeat." Margulan's three generals story is a direct Sun Tzu reference. |
| **Stoic concentration** | Seneca: "Nusquam est qui ubique est" — to be everywhere is to be nowhere. Concentration of effort is a Stoic virtue, not just a productivity technique. |
| **Tao Te Ching** | Chapter 16: "Return to the root." The strategic line is the *root* — the daily hustle is the branches. Without returning to the root periodically (review + re-alignment), the branches grow in all directions. |
| **Aristotle's practical wisdom (phronesis)** | The capacity to identify the right action in a particular situation. Phronesis is not knowing the rules — it is knowing which rule applies *here*, now. The key success factor question cultivates phronesis. |

## 5. Anti-patterns to Avoid

| Warning from transcript | App anti-pattern to avoid |
|---|---|
| "Busyness without strategy is bustle before failure" | Never reward task *volume*. No "you completed 15 tasks today!" metrics. |
| "A plan is a sequence. Strategy is a line of behavior" | Don't let the Horizon view be a flat list of goals. It should show *direction* and *posture*, not just deliverables. |
| "The balance wheel achieves nothing by itself — you must allocate time resources" | Don't show a balance wheel as a visualization without linking it to time allocation. A pretty wheel with no block assignment is decorative. |
| "20% of actions create 80% of results — the rest should be reduced" | Don't make it equally easy to add any kind of task. Surface the "is this high-leverage?" question at task creation time. |
| "Finding your point of application is more important than working harder" | Never gamify effort quantity (hours logged, tasks completed). Gamify *leverage*: decisions made, bottlenecks removed, reviews conducted. |

### Additional (psychology-based)
- **Planning fallacy** (Kahneman): People systematically underestimate how long tasks will take and overestimate what they can fit in a week. The time cluster approach forces a *capacity reality check* — you literally cannot fit more blocks than there are hours.
- **Goal substitution**: Easy tasks crowd out important ones when they share a list. The separate "deep work cluster" protects the high-value work from the administrative noise.

## 6. Voice & Tone Notes

### For Strategy screens

**When a user hasn't defined a strategy for a role:**
- ✓ "What's your line of behavior for [role] this season? One sentence is enough."
- ✗ "Add a goal" (this jumps past strategy to execution)

**When showing the Horizon view:**
- ✓ "What conditions are you shaping this quarter?" (General 3 framing)
- ✗ "What are your targets?" (output framing without strategy)

**When reviewing the 80/20 at month-end:**
- ✓ "Which of your actions actually moved the needle this month? What would you stop?"
- ✗ "You completed X tasks" (activity framing)

**When a high-leverage task keeps getting deferred:**
- ✓ "This one keeps waiting. Is there a real obstacle? Or can we make it smaller?"
- ✗ "This task is 14 days overdue"

### Margulan's strategic tone
He speaks with the certainty of someone who learned from expensive mistakes, not from books. He never moralizes. His framing is always *practical* and *experiential*:
> *"I didn't read this in a book — I lost money because I didn't have this. That's why I'm sharing it."*

The app should carry a similar tone: *"Here's what actually works. Here's what gets in the way. You decide."*
